# Data governance and access

For every real source record the steward, stable URL, exact version/retrieval window, terms and license URL, permitted redistribution, derived-data rights, access procedure, retention/update policy, and correction or removal contact. A source being publicly visible does not establish redistribution rights. Keep evidence of the decision and consult the supervisor when institutional review or source permission is needed.

On-chain records can expose pseudonymous activity; linking them to off-chain organizations or people changes what can be inferred. Describe the intended linkage level and avoid claiming identity or control without suitable evidence. Assess whether integrated exports reveal more than either component individually, and publish a reduced or controlled-access form where the actual risk and permissions warrant it.

For off-chain sources distinguish original materials from extracted features, copyrighted text, annotations, and third-party labels. Record language and geographic coverage, access barriers, survivorship, source revisions and known measurement limits. If people or annotators are involved, document applicable ethics decisions, consent, recruitment, compensation and review procedures.

Store credentials in local environment variables or Colab Secrets and remove them from queries, logs and outputs. The bundled example is offline and requires none. Approve specific release files, not entire working directories. Record source-specific licenses; the repository's MIT code license does not grant rights to external data.
